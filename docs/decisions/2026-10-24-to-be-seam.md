# One seam: presentation reaches the lab only through `/api` or gateway MCP

- **Date:** 24 Oct 2026
- **Status:** accepted; implemented on `arch/to-be-seam`
- **Context:** the TO-BE section of `CLAUDE.md` (onion inside hexagonal, one low-code/pro-code seam)

## Decision

1. **Presentation is a client of the seam.** The review app and the fabric's Teams tab reach the lab
   only through `/api` (Entra app roles, enforced at the gateway) or gateway MCP. Neither holds a
   Redis, store, database or MCP secret. The rule is enforced by
   `tests/governance/test_presentation_uses_the_seam.py`.
2. **The review app is a relay.** It authenticates as its own Entra registration
   (`REVIEW_ENTRA_CLIENT_ID`, holding `Workflow.Submit`, `Approvals.Read` and `Approvals.Decide`) and
   passes the signed-in reviewer as `actor` / `requester`. Decisions record as `api:review-app`.
3. **A person may call `/api` with a delegated token**, under two conditions:
   - they (or a group they're in) hold the operation's app role;
   - the gateway checks the body's `actor` (decide) or `requester` (submit) against the token.

   The reason: a Power Platform custom connector can carry only a person's token, because connectors
   have no client-credentials grant. Before this decision, delegated tokens were refused on `/api`.
4. **Bytes are forwarded, not fetched.** `GET /api/artifacts` and `POST /api/uploads` are forwarded by
   the front door to storage-mcp over the private network. storage-mcp holds the store credentials;
   the front door still holds Redis only.
5. **Channels are inbound adapters, not presentation.** They consume `approvals:requests` and relay a
   person's answer, so they may drive the approval API and the streams. They must not reach a store.

## Consequences

- A low-code surface can do everything the review app does, holding the same roles. The connector is
  imported from `GET /api/openapi.json`, which is generated from the process registry and the policy
  table.
- **Better audit for connector calls:** a connector call is attributable to the person whose token it
  carries. A relay's actor, by contrast, is asserted by the app.
- **Accepted cost:** every review-app page load is now an HTTP round trip through the gateway, not a
  Redis read.
- **Remaining debt (ratcheted):**
  - the meeting app's channel writes lane comparisons into the store;
  - its tab serves stored files as bytes, and no gateway tool returns bytes.
- **Migration:** APIM's `validate-jwt` with `<required-claims>` covers both token shapes. The
  actor-equals-token check becomes a policy expression on the decide and submit operations.
