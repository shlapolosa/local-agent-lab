| A capability is | A capability is not |
|---|---|
| An ability the business possesses — a stable noun phrase, "Manage Delivery Exceptions" | A department . If renaming a team changes the map, you have mapped the org chart |
| A bundle of people, process, information and technology | A process . Processes are how and in what order; capabilities are what, and are order-independent |
| Stable across reorganisation and outsourcing | An application . If replacing a system changes the map, you have mapped the estate |
| Realised by one or more business functions | A project or an initiative |

# Business capability map rules — levels

| Level | What it is | Typical count | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Domain | A broad area of the business — Supply Chain, Customer Service, Clinical Operations | 8–15 for most enterprises | Would survive a merger unchanged |
| L2 Capability | A named ability within a domain — Manage Delivery Exceptions | 4–10 per L1 | Owned by one accountable executive |
| L3 Sub-capability | The level at which investment is actually decided — Detect Delay, Assess Contractual Exposure | 3–8 per L2 | The stopping rule: decompose until further decomposition stops changing an investment decision, then stop |
