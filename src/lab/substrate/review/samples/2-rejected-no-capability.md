# On-site data-centre hardware swap rota

## The problem
When a disk or power supply fails in the on-premises data centre, a technician must physically
attend the rack, remove the failed unit, fit the replacement from the spares cage, and update the
asset register. Failures run at roughly one a week. The attending technician is chosen by whoever
is on shift, which means the same two people take most of them.

The work itself is unavoidable and takes about three hours door to door, almost all of it spent on
the floor with the hardware. There is no queue and no backlog: a failure is attended the same day.

## Who has it
Two data-centre technicians carry the rota. The Facilities Manager answers for the site.

## What changes if this works
The rota is shared evenly rather than falling to whoever is nearest, and the asset register is
updated from the work order rather than from memory afterwards.

## Accountable owner
Priya Raghunathan, Facilities Manager.

## What we already know
- The three hours are hands-on physical work: removing and fitting hardware in a locked facility.
- There is no decision to make and no document to read — the failed unit is identified by the
  monitoring system before anyone attends.
- Spares are held on site; procurement is not involved.
