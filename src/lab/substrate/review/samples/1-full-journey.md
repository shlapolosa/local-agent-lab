# Prior-authorisation clinical review backlog

## The problem
Requests for prior authorisation arrive from providers as PDF packets — a referral letter, recent
labs, and sometimes imaging reports. Four clinical reviewers read each packet end to end to decide
whether the request meets the published medical-necessity criteria. The packets arrive unevenly:
around 320 a week, with Monday carrying roughly a third of them.

Two things go wrong. Reviewers spend most of each packet locating the handful of facts the criteria
actually turn on, rather than applying judgement to them. And because the criteria live in a
60-page document that is revised quarterly, reviewers working from memory apply superseded versions
— an internal audit last quarter found this in 11% of a 200-case sample.

## Who has it
The clinical review team (four reviewers and their team lead) carry the daily load. Providers feel
it as turnaround time: the published service standard is three working days and the team currently
averages 4.6, with Monday arrivals waiting longest. The Head of Utilisation Management answers for
the service standard.

## What changes if this works
Reviewers see the criteria that apply to the request in front of them, with the relevant facts from
the packet located and cited back to the page they came from. The reviewer still decides — the
decision is clinical and stays with a clinician. Turnaround returns inside three working days
without adding headcount, and criteria-version errors become visible rather than silent, because
every decision records which version it was made against.

## Accountable owner
Dr Amina Farouk, Head of Utilisation Management.

## What we already know
- Criteria documents are maintained in SharePoint and versioned; the current version is always known.
- Packets contain personal health information and never leave the tenancy.
- A decision that is wrong in the provider's favour is a financial loss; one wrong in the other
  direction delays care. The second is the one that matters.
